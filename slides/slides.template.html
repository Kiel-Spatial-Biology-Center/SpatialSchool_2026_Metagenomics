Where do gut bacteria actually live?

Micro-scale spatial metagenomics: measuring the microbiome of a chicken gut at a resolution of a few hundred micrometres — and testing whether what we find is really structured in space.

The question
Bacteria in the gut are not randomly mixed. Do communities a few hundred micrometres apart differ systematically — and can we say which bacteria sit at each end of the gradient?

Conventional metagenomics cannot ask this. Homogenising a caecum destroys every spatial relationship in it before sequencing begins. To ask where, the sample has to stay intact and be taken apart under a microscope.

MSSM does not pool libraries equimolarly

Standard metagenomics

Quantify each library, pool equimolarly.
Every sample receives roughly equal reads.

Depth is a nuisance — deliberately equalised, and any residual variation is something to correct for.

MSSM

Equal tissue area, equal PCR cycles, pooled as they come. One cryosection per sequencing run.

Depth is a measurement — reads track the DNA that went in, and therefore the microbial biomass.

Two caveats. Depth is a share of one run, so it is relative within a cryosection and never comparable across them — which is why every analysis stays inside one section. And it is a proxy: extraction and PCR efficiency vary, and deeper sequencing detects more, so richness inherits both signals.

Chapter 1 · from mapping output to a community table

Count coverage, not reads

At face value, nearly every genome in the catalogue appears in nearly every microsample — a median of 222 of 223. That cannot be real for a fragment of tissue this small.

Mapping is a heuristic: reads from a genome that is present also align, imperfectly, to its relatives. Those stray reads scatter across the wrong genome instead of covering it evenly.

So ask how much of the genome was covered, not how many reads arrived. Breadth of coverage is strongly bimodal, which is what makes a threshold defensible.

The filter discards most cells and almost no reads. What it removes was near-empty to begin with — the rare tail that was mostly mapping noise.

Chapter 1 · a confound you cannot remove

Richness rides on sequencing depth

Spearman ρ = 0.94 in the caecum, 0.93 in the colon, between richness and log sequencing depth.

Two mechanisms produce that, and this dataset cannot separate them:

  • bioA microsample with more biomass genuinely holds more genomes.
  • techA microsample sequenced more deeply detects more of whatever it holds.

Both are real. Neither can be switched off. Any claim resting on richness alone is on thin ice.

Why this is not fatal

Because of the pooling protocol, depth is not a nuisance to be regressed away — it is a biological variable in its own right.

The analysis in Chapter 3 does not try to remove it. It gives it an axis of its own, so that the other axes can be read cleanly.

Chapter 2 · testing for spatial structure

Two questions the raw table mixes together

How much was there?

Sequencing depth. Under this protocol, a stand-in for biomass.

Set aside here, deliberately — and picked up again in Chapter 3 as its own variable.

What was it made of?

Genuinely compositional: within a microsample only the ratios between genomes are interpretable.

Handled with a centred log-ratio transform — divide by the microsample's geometric mean, then take logs.

Note what CLR does to the total: it divides it out entirely. That is the right move for asking what lives somewhere, and it is exactly why the biomass signal has to be reintroduced separately later.

The case for micro-scale sampling

Homogenise a caecum and you average away almost everything that is going on inside it.

It also tells us where to work: inside one cryosection, where gut region, animal and laboratory batch are all held constant and only position varies.

Chapter 2 · spatial autocorrelation

Moran's I — a correlation coefficient for space

Microsamples are not on a grid, so "nearby" is defined by a Gabriel graph: two microsamples are joined when no third lies inside the circle having them as its diameter.

A result in its own right

Sequencing depth is spatially autocorrelated. Read as biomass, that means microbial biomass is patchy at the sub-millimetre scale — neighbouring pieces of tissue tend to be densely or sparsely colonised together.

And a puzzle

Richness correlates with depth at ρ > 0.9, yet is spatially flat. Two variables can be tightly correlated and still behave differently in space.

Chapter 3 · extended RLQ

Ask for covariance, not variance

Stop looking for the axis with the most variance. Look for the axis along which what we know about microsamples and what we know about genomes covary. Neither noise nor biomass can fake that: there is no reason for a microsample's DNA yield to correlate with what its bacteria can metabolise.

Classical RLQ links three tables — environment, community, traits. The extended version (Pavoine et al. 2011) adds space and phylogeny. Two axes carry 86 % of the co-inertia here.

A coherent story

Complex plant polysaccharides arrive in the lumen, and degrading them needs large gene repertoires. Close to the epithelium the substrates are host-derived instead, and the bacteria there are smaller and differently equipped.

Inherited, too

Blomberg's K = 1.52, p = 0.001. Closely related genomes sit at the same end of the gradient more than a random walk along the tree would produce — spatial preference is phylogenetically conserved.

What to take away

Three lessons

1 · The obvious method gave the wrong answer

PCA and PERMANOVA reported no spatial structure. The gradient was on axis 3 all along. Ranking axes by size is a choice, and nothing guarantees the biggest axis is the interesting one.

2 · A protocol decision made the result findable

Because libraries were pooled without normalisation, biomass entered the analysis as a measured variable instead of hiding inside the composition. Under equimolar pooling the differences would have been flattened — while the variation they cause stayed in the community table, unlabelled and inseparable from the gradient.

3 · Negative results are results

The colon section shows no gradient. One animal, one section per region, a modest R². The honest version of this analysis reports all of that.
